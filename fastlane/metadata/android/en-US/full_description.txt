OffLok makes your phone its own DNS: the directory that turns a site's name into its address. It looks every answer up at the source, from the root of the Internet down: no provider, Google or Cloudflare in the middle to receive the list of every site and app you use.

<b>What it changes</b>
• Your privacy: no directory receives the list of your visits any more. Each server learns only its own part (the root sees "org", the .org registry sees "wikipedia.org"), and OffLok keeps no trace of what you look up.
• Your freedom: the answers come from each site's own servers, not from a middleman who decides for you what exists.
• Answers you can trust: when a name is signed (DNSSEC), OffLok checks the signature on your phone and turns a tampered answer away.
• An Internet that holds: your directory depends on no company. What it found stays in memory, even when a server stops answering.
• Fewer trackers, if you want: blocking trackers and malicious sites is included, off at first: one switch per category, your own lists, a 10-minute pause, and "Trust this site".

<b>How it works on a phone</b>
Android lets an app answer the phone's DNS only through its VPN service. OffLok's VPN carries DNS queries and nothing else: browsing and every other connection go on as usual, neither slowed down nor seen by OffLok. The resolver itself is Unbound, by NLnet Labs, inside the app.
When the root servers are out of reach (a hotel's Wi-Fi), OffLok falls back on the network's DNS and says so; strict mode never does, and "Sign in to this network" lets a captive portal's page open.

<b>What OffLok does not do</b>
• It does not encrypt your queries: like all classic DNS, they travel in the clear. Your provider can still read them on the way; it just no longer receives them to answer them.
• It is not a VPN that hides you: sites see your address.
• Android runs one VPN at a time: OffLok cannot run next to another VPN app.
• A Private DNS hostname set in Android's settings sends every query around OffLok: the app tells you.

Free and open source (MIT), no account, no statistics sent. In English and French.
